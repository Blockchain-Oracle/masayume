# PRD Quality Review — Masayume

## Overall verdict

This is a genuinely strong PRD: it has a distinctive thesis ("A primitive is not a product"), decisions stated as decisions with rejected alternatives on record, testable consequences on nearly all 39 FRs, counter-metrics, and glossary discipline that downstream workflows can source-extract from cleanly. What's at risk is concentrated, not diffuse: an unexamined tension between SM-3 ("un-fakeable traction") and SM-C3 (no synthetic numbers) when the product itself operates house Runners; a provably-fair story with one unnamed exception (server-attested parlay pricing); and two copilot FRs (FR-24, FR-25) whose behavioral thresholds are unspecified where every other FR gives numbers. None of these blocks downstream work from starting; all four mediums should be resolved before or during architecture.

## Decision-readiness — strong

Decisions are stated as decisions throughout, with what was given up on record: "Privy rejected" with a specific failure-class rationale (addendum §A), "TEE/enclave attestation… Cut on merit" (§9), "No X (Twitter) rail in MVP" with the reuse path named (§9, addendum §G's "do not silently resurrect" list). The Open Questions (§14) are actually open — each is routed to a phase or owner ("Owner: Abu, decide before submission"; "an architecture decision; the FR-4 capability contract stands regardless") rather than answered in the next sentence. Counter-metrics (SM-C1…C3) are the clearest tell that trade-offs were confronted rather than smoothed: "Brake interventions firing is *success*, not lost volume" is a real position, not a balance.

The full-scope MVP (§10: "deadline is never a scoping argument") complies with an explicit user directive and is not treated here as a scoping dodge; the PRD does name the demo-critical path (§10.1 item 1) and de-risks via build ordering (addendum §I). Two genuine tensions did get smoothed, though — both below.

### Findings
- **medium** SM-3 and SM-C3 collide on house-Runner activity (§13 SM-3/SM-C3, §5.9) — SM-3 demands "≥200 settled positions, ≥25 unique wallets… seeded by real community use," and SM-C3 forbids padding `/stats` with "synthetic numbers." But FR-32 house Runners and the seed-liquidity script (§10.1.8) generate real on-chain settled positions that are self-originated. The PRD never says whether house-originated volume counts toward SM-3 — a skeptical judge (the product's own Tunde, UJ-3) will ask exactly this. *Fix:* define SM-3's inclusion rule explicitly (e.g., house/runner wallets disclosed and excluded from the headline count, or counted in a labelled separate row on `/stats`).
- **medium** The provably-fair story has an unnamed exception: parlay pricing (§5.6 vs. FR-33; addendum §C "Leg probabilities read from CLOB mid at open (server-attested)") — the trust engine's claim is "every stat is un-fakeable," but a parlay's combined odds rest on a server-attested read of the book at open. The escrow makes the *payout* trustless; the *price* is not, and the PRD doesn't surface that trade-off anywhere in the narrative. *Fix:* name it — either in §5.6 as a stated boundary of the trust layer or as an FR-33 consequence (e.g., the attested mids are published on the Parlay's Receipt so they're checkable after the fact).
- **low** Session-key fallback silently changes the funding surface (FR-4 [ASSUMPTION], §14 Q5, §15) — the fallback ("local session signer funded from the Vault") preserves the capability contract but moves tap-trading money from wallet to Vault, changing what Ada must do before her first popup-free bet and what her Portfolio shows. *Fix:* one sentence acknowledging the UX consequence of the fallback path so UX/architecture don't discover it independently.

## Substance over theater — strong

Nothing here reads as furniture. The Vision (§1) could not swap into another PRD in this category — "every sharp edge of the protocol (winnings must be claimed, windows expire and respawn, pools are recycled) is passed straight through to the user" is earned specificity, and the differentiation claims are sourced ("DreamDEX's own FAQ admits volume is not shown," §2). The NFRs are the opposite of boilerplate: NFR-5 carries product-specific numbers (quote debounce ~350ms, re-quote ~12s), NFR-2 binds a named gotcha canon, NFR-9 turns the honest-state discipline into an accessibility requirement.

The nine UJs exceed the usual persona-count flag, but each one is load-bearing: every UJ maps to a distinct feature cluster and is cited by FRs ("Realizes UJ-x"), and UJ-9 (the judge) is a legitimately clever move — internalizing the evaluator as a user drives FR-38, NFR-8, and the fixture pages. The one whiff of future-feature framing — §5.9's "marketplace *language* (verification ladder, decision envelope)… without building creator self-serve" — is explicitly labelled as framing, which is the honest version of that move. No findings.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The thesis is stated and bet on: the venue calls itself agents-first and no EC-native consumer or agent experience exists (§1); every feature cluster demonstrates one face of "what the primitive becomes" — consumer (Markets/Reels), trust (Provably-Fair), social (Takes), agents (Vaults/Executor/session keys). Success metrics validate the thesis rather than measuring activity: SM-1 is the live loop, SM-2 is technical depth verifiable on-chain, and the counter-metrics guard the thesis's integrity (SM-C1 explicitly refuses to optimize bet frequency against the Brake). Feature-to-judging-criteria mapping (§2's weights → §13's "Targets the 25% Technical criterion") gives the breadth a scope logic that matches the demonstration thesis.

### Findings
- **low** Social cold-start is unaddressed (§5.4, §5.3) — the Takes thesis ("a social post that IS liquidity") and the Reels interleave assume Takes exist, but the seed plan (§10.1.8) seeds *books*, not *posts*, and the realistic wallet count is ~25 (SM-3). The feed degrades gracefully to market cards, so this isn't broken — but the moat claim rests on a surface that may be empty at judging time. *Fix:* either a Takes seeding note (house accounts posting backed Takes, disclosed) or an explicit acceptance that launch Reels are market-card-dominant.

## Done-ness clarity — strong

This PRD sets a high bar on the dimension that matters most downstream. Nearly every FR carries genuinely testable consequences with numbers: "≤2 prompts from tapping Connect" (FR-1), odds "clamped [1,99]" (FR-8), "a fill above the cap is impossible" (FR-9), "Profit factor is null before the first loss" (FR-20), "a Runner tx exceeding any cap reverts" (FR-29), the formal no-divert statement (FR-30). Even the LLM surface gets a real acceptance mechanism: SM-5's scripted eval set with "100% of Baku Reads contain only snapshot-grounded numbers" turns "honest reason" from an adjective into a test. Vague-phrase scan ("gracefully," "reasonable," "user-friendly") comes up empty.

The exceptions cluster in the Baku feature, where the PRD's own numbers-everywhere standard makes the gaps visible.

### Findings
- **medium** FR-24 Brake triggers have no thresholds (§5.7, Glossary "Brake") — "Loss streak + stake escalation + rapid-fire prompting (restless signal)" names the signals but nowhere defines them: how many losses, what escalation ratio, what prompt rate. Addendum §E specifies the regime manager and circuit breaker in detail but not tilt *detection*. An engineer cannot write the FR-24 test, and UJ-4 (Maria) is the emotional centerpiece of the product. *Fix:* add tilt-detection defaults to the Numbers bank (addendum §F), tunable at build.
- **medium** FR-25's only consequence is the failure mode (§5.7) — "Memory failures degrade to a memory-less Read" is testable, but the positive behavior ("remembers per-user context… to enrich Reads") has no observable consequence: what is remembered, and how does a Read with memory differ from one without? *Fix:* one testable consequence, e.g., "a returning user's Read can reference their prior session's positions/patterns; the referenced facts must exist in the memory store."
- **low** Daily Stop is undefined in two places engineers will ask about (Glossary "Daily Stop," FR-24) — no default value (user-set only? opt-out?) and "midnight reset" names no timezone (user-local vs. UTC changes the test). *Fix:* specify both.
- **low** NFR-5's headline is an adjective (§11) — "fast enough for a 60s window to be tradeable" is supported by debounce/cadence numbers but no round-trip bound; "sub-second chain reads leveraged" describes the chain, not a requirement. *Fix:* a p95 quote-round-trip bound (even a generous one).
- **low** FR-2 consequence is garbled (§5.1) — "the app never lets a gasless-in-tUSDC-but-gasless-in-STT user hit a raw revert" presumably means *funded*-in-tUSDC-but-gasless-in-STT; as written the sentence inverts its meaning inside a testable consequence. *Fix:* reword.

## Scope honesty — strong

Omissions are explicit and argued, not inferred: §9 Non-Goals annotates cuts with "(Cut on merit, not schedule.)", §10.2 states *why* each item is out (bet-gated rooms "adds liability without a distinct job"), and addendum §G preserves rejected alternatives with rationale so they can't be silently resurrected. Assumptions are tagged inline and indexed (§15); the load-bearing ones (STT gas path, session-key implementability) carry named fallbacks. Open-items density — 8 Open Questions, 6 indexed assumptions, 1 `[NOTE FOR PM]` — is on the high side for a green-light PRD, but every item is routed to a phase or owner and none blocks the demo-critical path, so it reads as honest bookkeeping rather than deferred thinking.

### Findings
- **low** UJ-8 skips its own prerequisite (§3.3) — Nia's journey reads as chat-native ("She DMs the Masayume bot, links her account… places the bet from her vault"), but the Executor spends *Vault* balance (FR-36), and depositing requires the web app. The journey silently assumes a funded Vault. *Fix:* one clause in UJ-8 (or an FR-36 consequence: an unfunded-Vault bet request is refused with a deposit link).

## Downstream usability — strong

This is a chain-top PRD and it behaves like one. The Glossary (§4) opens with "Downstream artifacts use these terms verbatim" and the document keeps its own rule: Market/Window/Cadence/Side/Stake/Ticket/Take/Verdict/Receipt/Vault/Caps/Runner/Executor are used identically across UJs, FRs, SMs, and NFRs — no drift found. IDs are contiguous and unique (FR-1…39, UJ-1…9, SM-1…6 + SM-C1…3, NFR-1…9); every cross-reference checked resolves (FR-13→FR-8/9, FR-37→FR-9, §12→FR-28/30, SM validation lists→FRs). Every FR names its UJ inline, so sections extract standalone. All UJs have named protagonists; UJ-9's anonymous "judge" is deliberate and appropriate. The PRD/addendum split is itself a downstream-usability feature: technology decisions, numbers bank, and the gotcha canon live where architecture will look for them without polluting the product contract. No findings; small items in Mechanical notes.

## Shape fit — strong

Consumer product with meaningful UX plus an agent platform, feeding UX → architecture → stories: UJs with named protagonists are load-bearing and present; capability-spec rigor appears exactly where the product is a platform (FR-28…32's contract-level invariants); the hackathon context is absorbed into the product's own shape (judge as persona, NFR-8, fixture pages, submission deliverables listed as non-product in §10.1.8) rather than bolted on. Neither over- nor under-formalized. Brownfield-adjacent references (the Yosuku reference analysis, ported engines) are consistently framed as *reference*, not existing code, which matches reality. No findings.

- **§0 self-description is slightly inaccurate**: "FRs cite them where mechanism detail lives" — FR bodies carry no `context/` citations; the addendum carries them. Either soften §0 or accept the addendum as the citation layer (it works fine as-is; just don't let downstream go hunting for citations in FRs).
- **FR-4 appears twice in §10.1** (items 1 and 6). Intentional as sequencing, but epics/stories generation should dedupe it to one owning epic.
- **Addendum §F flags its own reconciliation item** ("no-entry cutoff… reconcile with gotcha #9 headroom at build") — carry this into architecture explicitly so it isn't lost.
- Required sections for a launch-stakes consumer PRD are all present: Vision, Why Now, Target User with UJs, Glossary, FRs, IA, Non-Goals, MVP scope, NFRs, Constraints, SMs with counter-metrics, Open Questions, Assumptions Index.
